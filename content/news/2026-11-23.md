The UN climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye, ended on Sunday without a deal after countries split over whether to mention fossil fuels. Delegations from nearly 200 countries had negotiated for two weeks. The main decision text was rejected almost two days after the summit was due to close on Friday. That made COP31 the longest UN climate summit on record. Murat Kurum, Türkiye’s environment minister and the COP31 president, closed the session without an agreement.

The final draft did not name oil, gas or coal. The European Union formally objected. Ireland’s climate minister Darragh O’Brien, speaking for the bloc, said it could not back a text that did not even mention fossil fuels. Panama also rejected the draft. Developing countries said it offered too little money to help them adapt to a warming climate. Australia’s Chris Bowen, who co-presided over the talks with Türkiye, said some parties held red lines that could not be met. The collapse does not cancel earlier commitments, including the 2023 pledge in Dubai to transition away from fossil fuels.

## Why it matters

This is today’s highest-impact story because the annual UN talks are the one forum where nearly every government negotiates climate action together, and this year they ended with no common text. The rejected draft itself acknowledged that warming is on course to exceed 1.5°C, the goal set in the Paris Agreement. UN Secretary-General António Guterres said he was deeply disappointed. “There is no ambiguity in the science,” he said. Still unclear is whether countries can settle the fight over fossil fuels and climate finance before next year’s summit.
