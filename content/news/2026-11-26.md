The Trump administration on Wednesday proposed new tariffs on goods from 14 economies, including China, the European Union, India and Taiwan. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer says they produce more factory goods than they need and export the surplus to markets where it hurts American industry. Chinese goods would face a new 20% tariff. A senior official said that, added to duties from Trump’s first term, the total on many Chinese goods would reach about 45%. Mexico and Norway were left out for now while their cases continue.

The plan follows investigations opened in March, weeks after the Supreme Court struck down Trump’s earlier country-by-country tariffs. Greer played down the impact. “When it all nets out, it really will just top up a few percentage points,” he told CNBC. The new rates would not stack on the separate 10% to 12.5% tariffs imposed over forced labor. Greer also proposed sparing about $30 billion of Chinese goods under the trade truce between Washington and Beijing. None of the duties is expected to take effect for at least a couple of months, after a public comment period and a hearing. China’s foreign ministry spokesman, Guo Jiakun, said Beijing opposed the move.

## Why it matters

This is today’s highest-impact story because it rebuilds broad US tariffs on most of America’s biggest trading partners, months after the Supreme Court threw out the last set. The duties would add costs for American importers and test fragile trade deals with China and Europe. The rates could still change after the comment period. Watch for whether China or the EU retaliates, and whether the US-China truce is extended before it expires in January.
