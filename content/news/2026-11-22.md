Mexican forces captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as “El Chapo Isidro” and one of the FBI’s ten most wanted fugitives, in Guasave, Sinaloa, on Friday. The raid left 11 people dead. Mexico’s Defense Ministry said four soldiers and seven suspected gunmen were killed, and eight people were detained. Meza Flores was flown to Mexico City and handed to federal prosecutors. The United States had offered a reward of up to $5 million for information leading to his capture.

Cartel gunmen answered the raid with roadblocks of burning vehicles and gunfire across Guasave. By Saturday, Sinaloa state officials said the situation was under control. Meza Flores was a leader of the Beltrán Leyva cartel and later ran his own organization. He is also described as an ally of Los Mayos, a faction of the Sinaloa Cartel. His cousin, said to be his deputy, and his brother-in-law, said to manage the group’s money, were arrested with him. Mexican officials said the operation drew on intelligence from the United States. US Ambassador Ronald Johnson praised the cooperation between the two countries.

## Why it matters

This is today’s highest-impact story because Meza Flores and his network are accused of smuggling drugs into the United States for years, where he faces drug trafficking charges. His capture removes one of the most wanted cartel figures on either side of the border. But it also tests whether Mexico can contain the reprisals in Sinaloa, a state already torn by cartel infighting. Watch whether the United States moves quickly to extradite him, and whether the violence in Guasave spreads in the coming days.
