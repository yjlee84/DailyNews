The International Court of Justice ruled on Thursday that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya Muslim minority in 2017. The judges voted 9 to 6. It is the first time the UN’s top court has found a state responsible for genocide. The court said evidence showed that Myanmar’s security forces killed about 10,000 Rohingya during the crackdown. More than 730,000 fled to Bangladesh.

Reading the judgment in The Hague, court president Yuji Iwasawa said Myanmar “has committed genocide against the Rohingya group in northern Rakhine state.” The court also found that Myanmar failed in its duty to prevent genocide. Myanmar has said the 2017 operation was a response to insurgent attacks on police posts. The judges ordered it to prosecute those responsible and to let the Rohingya return home safely. Myanmar must also pay compensation, with the amount to be set later. The Gambia brought the case in 2019. Britain said it would expel Myanmar’s defense attaché and called for an urgent meeting of the UN Security Council. Myanmar’s government did not immediately respond.

## Why it matters

This is today’s highest-impact story because the world’s highest court has found a state itself, not just individual officers, guilty of genocide. The finding cannot be appealed. About 1.2 million Rohingya still live in refugee camps in Bangladesh, and the ruling sets out what Myanmar owes them. But the court has no means of enforcing its judgments. A failure to comply can be taken to the Security Council. Watch whether the Council takes up the case, and how Myanmar’s government answers the order.
