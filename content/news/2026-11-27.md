Congo’s Ebola outbreak has passed 12,000 confirmed cases, government data showed on Thursday, as fighting in the east forced some health workers to flee. At least 12,021 infections and 5,904 deaths have been confirmed since the outbreak was declared in Ituri province in mid-May. The virus behind it is the Bundibugyo species, for which there is no approved vaccine or treatment. Cases have now been reported in eight of Congo’s 26 provinces. It is the deadliest outbreak in Congo’s history and the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record, after the 2014–2016 epidemic in West Africa.

Clashes between the army and the rebel Convention pour la révolution populaire (CRP) have flared again in Ituri, displacing civilians. Dr. Catherine Smallwood, the World Health Organization’s Ebola incident manager, said the fighting has kept teams from reaching some communities. It has also disrupted the monitoring of people exposed to the virus and left some treatment centres short of staff. On Tuesday, about a dozen people were evacuated to Bunia, the provincial capital, after being trapped for three days in a hospital in Akwe amid crossfire. The response was already struggling to keep track of the virus. Most new infections are found among people who were not on any list of known contacts, according to figures from the WHO and the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention. That means many chains of transmission go unseen.

## Why it matters

This is today’s highest-impact story because a virus with no vaccine or cure is spreading faster than responders can trace it, in a region where armed groups keep cutting off access. Nearly half of the confirmed patients have died. Containing Ebola depends on isolating the sick and following up their contacts, and the fighting makes both harder. Still unclear is how many cases and deaths go uncounted in areas teams cannot reach. Watch for whether the fighting in Ituri spreads and more health workers are pulled out.
