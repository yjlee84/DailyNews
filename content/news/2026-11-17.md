Congo’s Ebola outbreak has reached 11,376 confirmed cases and 5,568 deaths, the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control said in an update on Monday. Reuters reported that the toll has passed 11,000 infections and 5,500 deaths and is still rising about six months after the outbreak was declared.

The virus is the rare Bundibugyo species. The outbreak has spread to eight of Congo’s provinces, with Ituri in the east the worst hit, and cases have also been reported in Kenya and Nigeria. Close to half of the confirmed cases have ended in death. It is the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record, behind only the 2014 to 2016 epidemic in West Africa, which killed about 11,000 people.

No licensed vaccine or treatment exists for Bundibugyo virus disease, according to the ECDC. Insecurity has also slowed the response. Health workers and vehicles have come under attack and some communities distrust outsiders, Reuters reported. The Associated Press reported at the end of October that armed groups operate in border areas and that health workers have staged strikes over unpaid wages, closing some treatment centers.

## Why it matters

This is today’s highest-impact story because the outbreak kills close to half of those infected, has already crossed national borders, and has no licensed vaccine or treatment for this strain. It is also unfolding in a region where conflict and strikes limit the response. Still unclear is how many cases are going undetected and whether the strikes and insecurity will slow the response further.
