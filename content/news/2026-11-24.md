Nvidia’s board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president and chief executive on Monday, and the company’s shares closed up about 31%. Huang had resigned less than two weeks earlier after admitting extramarital affairs. One of them was with a woman US intelligence suspects of being a Chinese spy. The board said it had received input from senior levels of the US government and was confident his return posed no national security risk. Chief financial officer Colette Kress, who ran the company as interim chief executive, goes back to her finance role.

The rally spread across Wall Street. The Nasdaq Composite rose 6.5% and the S&P 500 gained 4.7%, after a selloff in technology stocks on Friday. Nvidia added close to $1 trillion in market value in a single day. “I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment,” Huang said. The decision reverses board chair Harvey Jones, who said last week that the board did not intend to bring him back. President Donald Trump called Huang a “national treasure” and said he “did nothing wrong.” Senator Elizabeth Warren called the decision reckless. An independent board committee is still investigating reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort.

## Why it matters

This is today’s highest-impact story because Nvidia is the main supplier of chips for artificial intelligence, and its shares carry heavy weight in the indexes that hold many people’s savings. A single board decision lifted Wall Street after a week of heavy losses. It also showed how far the White House is willing to step into the leadership of a private company. Huang is due to answer questions from three House committees behind closed doors on December 1. He is also due to testify in public before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3. Watch for what those hearings and the board’s own inquiry reveal about export controls and the spy concerns.
