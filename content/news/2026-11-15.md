President Donald Trump, China’s Xi Jinping and Russia’s Vladimir Putin will hold their first three-way meeting on Thursday, November 19, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, China, the White House said on Friday. Spokeswoman Olivia Wales said Trump would discuss reducing the world’s nuclear weapons and believes nuclear proliferation is “very dangerous.” The New START treaty, which capped the US and Russian strategic arsenals, lapsed in February. Trump rejected Russia’s offer to extend it and wants a “new, improved and modernized” agreement that includes China. A senior US official said Trump also intends to raise the war with Iran, but not Russia’s war in Ukraine.

Officials have played down expectations. A senior administration official told Reuters that the US does not expect any agreement and called the meeting the start of a conversation. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov called it “extremely significant,” rejected comparisons with the 1945 Yalta Conference and said agreements reached in Shenzhen could not be imposed on other countries. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has warned that the meeting could be used to discuss a Ukraine deal without Kyiv. Analysts quoted by the Associated Press expect a symbolic event rather than breakthroughs.

## Why it matters

This is today’s highest-impact story because the three leaders head the world’s largest nuclear powers, and since New START lapsed there has been no treaty limiting the two largest arsenals. Their agenda also includes the Iran war. What to watch: whether China agrees to any arms-control talks, whether Ukraine stays off the table as Washington says, and what, if anything, the three leaders announce on Thursday.
