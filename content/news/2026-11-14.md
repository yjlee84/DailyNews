Jensen Huang resigned on Thursday as chief executive of Nvidia, the chipmaker he co-founded and led for 33 years, and left its board with immediate effect. The company gave no reason. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress is serving as interim chief executive while keeping her finance role, and the board has begun a search for a permanent successor among internal and external candidates. Harvey Jones, a director since 1993, became the board’s first chair. Nvidia’s shares fell as much as 13% on Thursday before closing about 4% lower, then fell again on Friday, leaving the company’s market value below $4 trillion for the first time since July 2025. The stock lost more than 12% over the week. HSBC cut its rating to hold and its price target to $170 from $365, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.9% on Friday.

Huang’s exit followed a week of scrutiny. On Sunday he admitted an affair, and the board opened an inquiry into his relationship with a woman US intelligence reportedly believes was recruited by Chinese intelligence. The New York Times then reported an affair with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia’s vice president of health care, and Reuters reported another with an unnamed employee. Senate Banking Committee chair Tim Scott has asked Huang to testify publicly on December 3, and House Foreign Affairs chair Brian Mast said Huang had agreed to brief lawmakers, with the date still under negotiation.

## Why it matters

This is today’s highest-impact story because Nvidia’s chips underpin the boom in artificial intelligence, and the sudden loss of the founder who set its direction has shaken investors across the sector while Congress examines whether national security was put at risk. What to watch: Nvidia’s quarterly results on Tuesday, November 17, the first since Huang’s exit, who the board chooses as permanent chief executive, and what lawmakers ask Huang.
