Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said they launched six drones on Sunday at a US radar at the Al-Riffa helicopter base in Bahrain, the second attack on the Gulf kingdom in three days. Bahrain issued safety alerts, and its military said it intercepted the attacks before they reached their targets. On Friday, US Central Command said it shot down two Iranian drones aimed at a US early-warning radar in Bahrain, with no casualties or damage. Also on Sunday, the Guards said two oil tankers on an “unauthorised route” in the southern Strait of Hormuz hit naval mines, and that one, a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels of crude, sank. There was no independent confirmation. The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre said Indian shipping authorities had reported an attack on a tanker in the Gulf, with the crew safe and the damage unknown; it was unclear whether this was the same incident.

The USS Theodore Roosevelt entered the area of US Central Command on Sunday, a US official said, joining two carriers already there and giving the United States three in the region. Iran’s deputy foreign minister, Kazem Gharibabadi, told the state news agency IRNA that Tehran “will not wait for the US to attack first.” Bahrain’s foreign ministry held Iran fully responsible for both attacks. The war began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February, and months of diplomacy have not ended it. Oil barely moved: Brent crude traded below $96 a barrel early on Monday, as traders weighed the attacks against an International Energy Agency forecast that supply will exceed demand this quarter.

## Why it matters

This is today’s highest-impact story because the Iran war sets the price of oil, and through it fuel costs and inflation for most of the world, and Iran has resumed attacks on Bahrain just as the US adds a third carrier. What is still uncertain: whether the reported sinking is confirmed, whether either side resumes wider strikes, and whether this week’s meeting of Trump, Xi and Putin moves the war toward talks.
