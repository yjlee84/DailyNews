The United States has lost the measles elimination status it had held since 2000, the Pan American Health Organization said on Tuesday. The regional health body found that the virus had circulated inside the country without a break for more than a year. Mexico and Bolivia lost their status at the same time. More than 5,400 measles cases have been confirmed in the US this year, the most since 1991.

The decision followed a review by an independent expert commission that met in Washington from November 10 to 13. Canada lost its status last year. On Wednesday, Pennsylvania’s health department said an unvaccinated resident of Clinton County had died with measles. It is the state’s eighth measles death of 2026, and the state has confirmed 1,589 cases in 46 counties. Reported national death tolls range from six to ten, depending on the count used. Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., who has questioned the safety of vaccines, urged people to get the measles, mumps and rubella shot. The head of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, Erica Schwartz, played down the label itself. “The measles elimination designation is not what protects Americans. Vaccination does,” she said.

## Why it matters

This is today’s highest-impact story because the ruling means measles is once again treated as endemic in the US, a quarter of a century after it was declared eliminated. Two doses of the vaccine are 97% effective, and experts link the outbreaks to falling vaccination rates. The US is expected to send the health organization a plan to stop the spread in the coming weeks. To win the status back, it must show that the virus no longer circulates. Watch for that plan, and for whether new cases in Pennsylvania begin to slow.
