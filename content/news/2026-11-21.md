The S&P 500 fell 3.7% on Friday and the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 949.73 points as investors dumped artificial intelligence stocks. Both indexes closed more than 10% below their record highs, the definition of a correction. The S&P 500 ended at 6,910 and the Dow at 48,147.33. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.28% to 22,617.73 and erased its gains for the year. The S&P 500 has now fallen for seven sessions in a row. It lost 8.6% this week, its worst week since April 2025.

Chipmakers led the losses. Nvidia fell nearly 14% after Reuters reported that the Securities and Exchange Commission is examining possible accounting issues at the company. An investigation is not a finding of wrongdoing. Anthropic rose 17% in its stock market debut on Thursday but gave back more than 8% on Friday. Bitcoin fell below $60,000. “The stock market is going through an AI repricing,” Richard Saperstein, chief investment officer at Treasury Partners, told CNBC. He pointed to slowing data center construction and less funding for AI projects. Markets are also pricing in a quarter-point interest rate rise by the Federal Reserve in December.

## Why it matters

This is today’s highest-impact story because AI and chip companies have carried much of the stock market’s rise, and the indexes that fell on Friday sit at the core of pension funds and retirement savings. A drop this fast suggests investors now doubt that the heavy spending on data centers will pay off soon. A rate rise in December would make that spending more expensive to finance. Watch whether the selloff spreads beyond technology stocks next week, and what the SEC inquiry into Nvidia turns up.
