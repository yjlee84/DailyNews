Nvidia’s revenue rose 103% from a year earlier to $115.8 billion in the quarter to October 25, well above the roughly $109 billion analysts had expected. Sales from data centers more than doubled to $107.6 billion. The company forecast about $130 billion for the current quarter, which has an extra week, and its shares rose about 5% in after-hours trading on Tuesday.

It was the chipmaker’s first report since Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive last week. The board has said he left over personal behavior that conflicted with its code of conduct. Before the results, Huang said he would be honored to return if the board wanted him. On the earnings call, board chair Harvey Jones shut that down. “The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO,” he said. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is running the company as interim chief executive while the board looks for a successor. Meanwhile, the Wall Street Journal reported that the Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia. The focus of that inquiry is not known.

## Why it matters

This is today’s highest-impact story because Nvidia’s results are read as a test of demand for artificial intelligence, and they move markets far beyond the company itself. The S&P 500 fell about 1% on Tuesday before the report, in a selloff led by technology stocks. The numbers show demand for its chips is still rising fast, even as the company hunts for a new leader and faces an SEC inquiry. Still unclear is who will lead Nvidia next, what the SEC is examining, and whether strong sales will be enough to steady investors while those questions stay open.
