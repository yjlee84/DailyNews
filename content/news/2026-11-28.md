Italy’s foreign minister, Antonio Tajani, said Kuwaiti investigators found that the drone which killed two Italian technicians in Kuwait on Thursday was made in Iran. The two men worked for Rheinmetall Italia, the Italian unit of the German defence group. They were maintaining the radar of a Skyguard air-defence system at a Kuwaiti military site when it was hit. Antonio Saccoccio was 65 and Emanuele Lo Russo was 36. A Kuwaiti army officer and a Pakistani national were also injured. “Following a powerful explosion, both of our compatriots, according to information coming from Kuwait, have died,” Tajani told the Italian Senate.

Kuwait’s Defence Ministry called the strike an Iranian attack. Iran denies it. Abolfazl Shekarchi, a spokesman for Iran’s armed forces, said Iranian forces had carried out no military operations in the region. Tajani said Iran’s foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, told him the drone was not launched from Iran or by its forces. Tehran has offered to join an inquiry with Italy and Kuwait. Tajani also cautioned that an Iranian-made drone was not necessarily fired by Iran, since militias backed by Tehran use the same weapons. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called for more pressure on Iran. The deaths came during a week of attacks across the Gulf. Houthi fighters from Yemen struck Saudi oil refineries, and the US military said it disabled another merchant ship trying to break its blockade of Iranian ports.

## Why it matters

This is today’s highest-impact story because a war that began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran in February is now killing citizens of European countries that are not fighting in it. Gulf states that host US bases have been hit repeatedly, and the attacks keep pressure on oil supplies and shipping through the region. Still unclear is who launched the drone and whether the joint inquiry will happen. Watch for whether Italy and the European Union move from condolences to sanctions or other measures against Tehran.
